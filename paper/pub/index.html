Who Decides What’s Worth Saving?

By Cynthia

I arrived in America wearing these shoes. They accompanied me on my travels and throughout my first year at SCU. By the time we moved, they were badly worn, but I wanted to keep them. My mother threw them away anyway. She’d done the same with all the shoes I’d worn before.

01

Seventeen Candles

Amsterdam journalist Martine Postma kept seeing discarded belongings on rubbish-collection days that might still have been usable. Finding someone to fix an old appliance had become harder than buying a replacement.

In 2009, she reported on Platform21’s Repair Manifesto, which argued that mending could add to an object’s history. Inspired, she began recruiting volunteer repairers, starting with a friend’s handy husband. A carpenter, a seamstress, and others followed.

On October 18, dozens arrived at Amsterdam’s Fijnhout Theatre with broken belongings. The idea brought together people who wanted to keep their possessions and people who enjoyed the challenge of fixing them.

By 2012, Repair Café had reached Palo Alto, where volunteers welcomed objects regardless of their age or condition, provided their owners wanted to keep them.

Exactly seventeen years later, on October 18, 2026, I visited Repair Café Silicon Valley in San José.

02

What Comes Through the Door

At Repair Café, visitors check in with their broken belongings and are matched with volunteers who might know how to fix them. Rather than leaving their things behind, owners are encouraged to stay, ask questions, and lend a hand.

03

At the Workbench

Screws and spare parts were meticulously sorted into labeled compartments, alongside more varieties of screwdriver bits than I knew existed. For all that preparation, the volunteers could never be quite sure what a repair would demand.

My stereo dated from August 2003. Its two units were connected by a cable containing twenty separate wires. At bench 2, the cover came off, and every wire was stripped and soldered back onto its pin by hand.

Aaron, one of the volunteers, liked that every repair was a little different. Even familiar problems could call for a new approach, and he enjoyed figuring out what might work when the usual methods didn’t quite fit.

04

The People at the Tables

At the sewing table, the repair tickets were remarkably specific: “Needle hits the foot” and “Tear in the inner thigh.”

05

The Two Clocks

At another table, two women examined an ornate, heart-shaped clock, with a flowered dial and gold roses around its frame. The clock face, hands, and numbered ring lifted away in layers; behind them were the clock movement and a small speaker assembly.

Jazz, a volunteer I spoke with, told me about another clock from an earlier Repair Café. A woman had brought in her late father’s cuckoo clock, which hadn’t worked in years. She wanted it running again because it reminded her of him.

“I had to be quite creative to repair it,” Jazz said.

The clock’s chains had fallen off the cogwheels inside its case. Getting them back into place was difficult. “I had to use a stiff wire to feed them back into the correct position,” he explained.

He secured the chains so they wouldn’t slip off again during the journey home. “I tied them up so they wouldn’t fall out again on the way home.” He told the woman to leave the ties in place until the clock had been mounted on her wall.

When the repair was finished, she asked whether she could hug him.

“She gave me the biggest hug!”

Weeks later, she returned to another Repair Café event and hugged him again.

“Do you think she was happy?” Jazz asked.

06

The Odds

In the 2019 RepairMonitor, 41% of clocks with recorded ages were over fifty years old. Three-quarters of coffee makers were less than ten years old. The report also found that objects made before 1980 appeared somewhat more likely to be repaired successfully than newer ones.

07

Why Bring It In?

At an earlier Palo Alto Repair Café, two engineers spent more than an hour working on a sixty-year-old Waring drink mixer. An identical model was listed online for $16.

In Palo Alto, the reasons ranged from cost to a husband’s heartbreak.

Methods & notes

Counts and odds. The counter and the drinks use worldwide figures from RepairMonitor’s 2019 analysis by the Repair Café International Foundation: 13,609 repairs logged at 67 cafés in eight countries. None of them are counts from the San José event. Clocks are grouped with the things that have no plug, as the report groups them.

The glass. The layers follow the Farnham Repair Café visitor survey, 2016, from the Centre for Sustainable Design. The scores are weighted rankings from a small sample. They are not percentages, so the layers show order and weight, nothing more.

Quotes. Jo Ann Arneson spoke to Verde Magazine in 2012. Tim Showalter, Ann Lemmenes and Bill Wall spoke to June Bell for “Getting Their Fix for Free,” Alta, August 2018.
